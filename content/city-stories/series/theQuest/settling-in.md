The bright sun took some of the chill out the day. The work of laying in several garden beds had produced a surprising number of large stones. Still more had been found in some of the  parks and along the shore. Earlier in the week, she stacked the rocks to form a large basin in the shadow of the old oak tree in the backyard. She worked clay  into the cracks and seams, smoothing it to form a large dish. After the clay had dried, under a tarp, she lit a fire in the dish and let it burn, feeding it until the clay and rocks glowed red. After a final feeding, she let the fire burn itself out. Cleaning up the ash, she found a smooth, hardened basin.

She gazed into the fire she had started earlier in the day, using the strong autumn sun to ignite the tinder. The ritual of focusing had been one of the first rituals she’d learned. It has focused the light of the sun into a tight beam that lit the tinder, in the same manner a magnifying class would. The wood had been gathered from her yard as she’d cleaned up years of neglect. She began the meditative breathing ritual that she’d learned as a child, back when it was a game, not a tool. Her mind, both drifting and focused began to fill. Her mind filled with the image of the flames and the feel of the breeze. Anima wandered the areas adjacent to the lightly warded property.

She could feel the thinness of the Gauntlet around her, thinner than most small towns. It was unimaginably thin for a large city. Spirits, both natural and malevolent prowled beyond her senses. On her side, she could feel the energy of Judas’s cats. She couldn’t feel him, but he’d left earlier. Myriad other forms of life abounded around her. The unawakened, animals of several types, and all the plant life. Breathing deeply, her mind surfaced from the deeper mediation.

Opening her eyes, she realized that hours had passed and the sky was nearly dark. The sliver of the moon hung barely above the horizon. She began arranging the herbs and other small branches and leaves that she’d gathered. As she picked up each handful, she examined it with both her physical and spiritual senses. She focused on it’s properties, what gift it provided, and how it fit her ritual of consecration.

She gathered the twigs of ash, oak, willow, and elder tree into bundles. She then wrapped with small withies made of hazel. She started a quiet chant while placing a small clay dish holding clear water over the fire. The layer of soot on the outside showed this was a familiar spot for the dish. As the chant continued, the water boiled and runes painted into the glaze of the dish began to glow. She  mixed the small bundles of herbs into the bot. As they steeped, steam lifted from the surface of the water.

Finishing her chant, she raised the dish up with one hand, casting the bundles of twigs into the fire. As the smoke from the branches rose, she sipped the hot liquid. With every sip, water rose in the rough basin in front of her, covering the small token she’d placed in the basin. When the dish was finished, the basin was full of clear water. For a moment, she could see the sun and the clouds reflected. As the reflection faded to the moon and stars, she drew the small iron blade, forged by her uncle. She used the tip to nick each finger of her left hand in order. The last one done, she flicked the first drops of blood into the fire and the second to the basin. As the drops hit the fire, smoke billowed and the flames leapt, as if she had poured gasoline on the flames. The flare up climbed higher. The impact of her blood on the water formed concentric circles. Her fingers healed as they lapped against the edges of the basin.

She focused her attention on the intersections of the ripples. Her mind drawn into the motion of the small waves. As the ripples stilled, the pool began to reflect the yard through a series of different lens. The yard as it was began to fade, the structures vanishing. Wild growth began to show, signs of hidden movement concealed by the growth. A great skull burst from the chaotic mass of green. Long curved tusks extending from the front of the skull. It’s color changing from a deep black to a cream color, then to the bright white of freshly exposed bone. The smell of blood filled the air.

As the smell of blood faded, the tusks moved to curve up an over the skull in a motion like a slow ballet. The sound of wolves howling echoed in her mind. The remaining black flowed and twisted. A burst of orange exploded, the skeletal remains transforming into a monarch butterfly. The wild green growth shimmered into the downy white of milkweed pods. The milkweed silk bean to twist and turn in place, transitioning into the woven strands of a spider web. The web began to glow with an unhealthy and unnatural light. The web began to expand, then faded to points of light in the ripples of the pool. Slowly, the pool stilled as Gillian’s mind returned from the trance. As she sat, the few stars that shown through the city lights began to reflect in the pool.

Slowly, quietly, reverently, she cleaned up her space. Ash and compostable materials headed to the new compost heap. The small scrying bowl was emptied and wiped out. The fire, extinguished with water from the basin. The small token, a carving of a child, returned to her necklace. Completing her tasks, she moved slowly inside to think about and record what her five senses had experienced. Still moving in a slow and deliberate manner, she thought about what the ritual had shown her. She’d hoped that guidance would come from the aftermath of the consecration. She’d received a gift beyond her wildest expectations. In the quiet, a small tendril of hope crept in, a path for her journey seen for the first time.
