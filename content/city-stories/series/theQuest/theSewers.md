The dark isn't quiet, the sounds of dripping and sometimes running water fill it. Near junctions or pumping stations, the noises change. In between, just the near silence of water itself.

A scraping sound, the loud clang of metal, and then the sound of falling debris hitting both something solid and water break the quiet. A few minutes later, the darkness is broken when a light assembly comes down the manhole shaft, lighting the narrow walkway and the stream of water next to it. Echoes carry for several minutes before voices start to drift down the ladder.

clump. scrap.
clump. scrap.

After a few minutes, a pair of knee-high rubber boots and faded yellow bib overalls that reflected brightly in the light came down the ladder. The first worker stepped off the ladder into a dank puddle. As he began rigging the lights, three more workers joined him. As the first worker rigged the light array, the second began flashing a light across the channel. 

"Manuel," he said, "seen his majesty yet?"

"No man," came the reply, "I haven't looked either. Mateo, what did your abuelo send today?"

"A bag of something, I didn't look. Abuelo wasn't happy I was down here today. He said something's hungry these days." Mateo replied.

"Well, there's plenty down here for whatever it is." Antonio replied.

"Stay focused, everybody got their gas meters on?" Lew said, "The shift's not long enough to fool around and there's been a lot of complaints about slow drains, God knows what's clogging something up."

At the edge of Manuel's light, two glowing eyes appeared, moving slowly in the noxious channel.

"Hey, El Viejo decided to show up!" Manuel called out. 

"You sure it's him?" Antonio asked, "I'd hate to give his cut to the wrong gator."

Manuel replied, "No other gator has eyes that big. It's got to be him."

"Well, make sure it's him and then toss it to him." Antonio instructed. 

"Quit fooling around with the damn gator." Lew snapped, "We need to get moving, time's burning."

As the eyes moved closer, the shape of a large almost albino alligator came into view. A massive snout, with a long scar running diagonally the length of it came into view first. The eyes followed with an enormous body trailing behind into the dark. Mateo opened the large plastic bag and took out a plastic wrap of meat. As he unwrapped the plastic wrap, he grimaced. 

"Man, that stinks, even down here." Mateo said as he finished removing the wrap. He tossed the several pounds of expired meat into the water in front of El Viejo's snout.

"Wonder who feeds him when we're not down here." Antonio asked, "He's come a long way since he was flushed."

"Not my problem," said Lew, "Clearing the line is." 

Antonio finished rigging the light array, pointing them down stream. Under the watchful eye of El Viejo, the rest gathered their gear and splashed through the small scum covered puddles on the walk way. 

After a few minutes of walking, lights shining out into the channel to check inflows, the crew paused. Lew confirmed the location tag on the wall. He shook his head and continued on. Periodically, Manuel would flash his light back, checking for a pair of glowing eyes. 

"Focus Manuel, you don't want to fall in. We'd fish you out, if the gator doesn't get you. God knows what you'd catch though." Lew said, "I don't need to catch the hell that would bring."

"Abuelo always said El Viejo was good luck. Nothing down here bothers anyone when he's around." Manuel replied, "It's important to teach that to the newbie. You learning, Mateo?"

"Bah" Lew grumbled, "We should almost be there. Better to teach him the safety sheet than to feed somebody's thrown away pet."

Two tags later, Antonio looks down and then sends his light further down the walkway. 

"Water's not puddles up ahead," he said, "it's level with the channel."

"Good, we're getting close to the blockage." Lew replied, "Let's keep moving, but carefully, no accidents."

Ahead, they could hear a small splashing noise, irregular, like a surge hitting something.

"That something splashing?" asked Mateo.

"Yeah, one of the giant fish that El Viejo eats." laughed Manuel, as he flashed his light back again.

"Wait," he said, "where's El Viejo?"

"You probably just missed him with the light," said Antonio, "he knows a good thing when it feeds him."

Lew sighed, "Not this shit again, as long as he stays well away, who cares. The plug must be around this corner."

Manuel's light flashed back and forth, for a moment, he could see the long tail moving side to side as El Viejo swam back the way they'd came.

"What the ..?" he said.

A moment later, the scream started.
