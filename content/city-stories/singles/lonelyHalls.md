"I'm not here."

"I'm NOT here."

"**I'M NOT HERE!**"

And he wasn't. The battle continued, where he had been, but he was gone. Much more gone than usual, which wouldn't be realized by others until the battle had passed.

It was black, pitch black. Somewhere he heard a slow dripping sound and the quiet buzz of fluorescent lights, but it was black that surrounded him as his mind slowly cleared. The quiet, even with the dripping sound, helped to calm his panicked mind.

"Slow, slow." he thought, "Breathe in, hold, breathe out."

No impacts, no new pain. His mind slowly calmed.

With calmness, came realization. The dark was because his eyes were closed. The concept of choice pushed the restarting of his mind. To open or not to open. A quiet giggle slid out of him. What would Shakespeare think of the rewording. Quiet laughter at himself followed.

Slowly he opened his eyes and quickly re-closed them. The bright white was blinding. Slowly he sat up, he didn't sense danger, but kept his guard up, listening carefully to what was going on around him. The quiet remained, not pressing, but omnipresent. He slowly reopened his eyes, allowing them to adjust.

Looking around, it wasn't as bright as he thought. There were fluorescent lights, the walls reflecting a brilliant sterile white, and a dressing room mirror on the wall. The room was small, a white door with a silver knob was in front of him.

Moving carefully, feeling every bruise and ache, he sat up. Too much practice had him checking himself for injuries. One slash through his jacket sleeve and several bruises were quickly found. A deep breath made his ribs ache.

"Ouch." he quietly gasped, "No deep breaths for a while."

His breath caught, his condition checked, now time to decide what to do next. The room itself was empty. The room was empty, nothing but him, some small smears of blood on the floor, and a door. Glancing down, the face of the cheap timex watch was shattered.

Rather than releasing the tension built up, panic started to build. This was not where he had been. He didn't feel safe; questions bombarded his mind - "How did he get here?", "Where was he?", and "Who's watching?" As his mind started to spiral and his vision darkened, he dropped to sit on the floor, his head between his hands. The light blocked, he started deep breathing. In for five, hold, out for five, repeat.

"I can feel the floor, my jeans, the pain in my ribs, the watch on my wrist, the tightness of my shoes on my feet." he continued, "I can smell the blood from my scratches, I can smell myself, I can smell the leather of my boots. Taste, my mouth tastes like something died in it. I can hear, I can hear .... not much."

Time passed. He'd been focused on his breathing, not time itself. He knew time had passed, but not how much. The room hadn't changed, nothing had come in or gone out. There was no indication of what the room was for, no contents to give context. He straightened up, his back aching from being hunched. He cautiously stretched, ribs were still tight and there were more smears on the floor. The scrapes and scratches had quit bleeding, but the wet clothing pulled across them uncomfortably. Standing he straightened himself up as best he could, checking his pockets as he shifted clothing to more comfortable positions. Finding his pager in a pocket, he smiled. 

The smile quickly disappeared though. The screen of the pager was blank and adjusting the battery didn't bring it back to life. Similarly, the old walkman in another pocket showed no signs of life.

"What are the odds?" he quietly wondered. He remembered using the Walkman earlier in the evening. He'd scanned the dials looking for the minds around him before they went over the fence. It has worked then and the power had started his inspection in the off position.

Pulling himself together, he went to turn off the light in the room. He was going to have to open the door, at least a little bit. Being backlit was a bad idea - he'd seen that in action earlier in the evening. He was really hoping that when he opened the door a large hairy arm wouldn't force it further and then yank him out. It happened to a guard and all the body armor hadn't helped at all. He'd seen lobsters take longer to crack and peel. 

After looking twice, he confirmed that no light switch had been installed in the room. Unhappily he contemplated his options. After careful thought, he slid behind the door and tried the knob. He was happy to not hear any noise and to feel it freely turn in his hand. Slowly he cracked the door open, carefully looking around it to see beyond it.

Nothing with teeth, nothing without teeth. He could see a short distance down a hallway. The identical fluorescent fixtures lined the ceilings and the walls and floor seemed to be the same material as the room he was in. He listened carefully, nothing. The faint dripping had vanished, unnoticed, but the slight buzz of the lights was still there. Easing the door more open, he looked the other way. No difference, in both directions, the hallway extended twenty to thirty feet, with a few doors on either side before a t intersection. No visible signs or markers on or by the doors.

"Great," he sighed, "no exit signs in sight. At least there's no trouble in sight either. Now, what to do."

He thought hard about closing the door and waiting to see what happened, but the silence and sense of emptiness led him to think that it might be a long time before something happened. Mentally, he ran over his options. Searching with the Walkman was out since the batteries were dead. He could stay where he was or explore. He wondered how long before he got thirsty or hungry. He didn't need a bathroom at the moment, but he was sure the time would come. If nothing else, he'd like to wash up.

Stepping back into the room, he left the door cracked, allowing him to listen for others. He looked around at the spotless floor and decided. More time sitting on the floor would get him nowhere, it was time to move. He took a deep breath, turned around and opened the closed door. Stepping out into the hallway, he pulled a silver dollar from his pocket. 

"Heads to the left, tails to the right." he quietly said to himself, flipping the coin.

And missed the catch. The coin made a quiet noise as it bounced off the floor and rolled for a moment. He stepped over to pick it up and his toe moved far enough to kick the coin a little ways down the hall. With a sigh, he listened for a moment, nothing. He went to step over to the coin and it wasn't there. He paused, looked again. Nothing. He carefully scanned the hall, no coin.

He took a step back to re-scan the hallway from where he had been. As his foot came down, he felt something under his foot move. He lifted his foot and saw the coin. Where it landed, not where it had been kicked to. Slowly, he reached down and picked up the coin. A slight shiver went through his body as he wondered. 

Flipping the coin again, he made the catch and checked the results. Heads, he turned to go back past the room he had come out of. He started to walk toward the intersection ahead but stopped after a few steps. He looked back and realized that the hallway looked exactly the same as far as he could see. The hair on the back of his neck rose. He'd left the door to "his" room open. All he could see was closed doors. He'd heard no clicks, no sounds. 

He paused, listening for a moment. No noise, just the barely audible hum of the ventilation system. He looked both ways, carefully examining the hallway itself and the doors. Nothing out of place, nothing different, just the rows of doors down to the intersections at each end of the hallway. He listened for a moment more as he thought. Deciding on a direction, he cautiously, almost on tip-toes moved in the chosen direction. 

After a few steps he spun around. Nothing sneaking up on him, no change in the ambient noise. He listened again, still nothing. He continued, cautiously until he was almost at the intersection. Again, he paused, listening. Then carefully looked around the corner. He quickly glanced to the left and spun to the right. The same sterile walls and harsh fluorescent lights stretched in both directions. As when he started, he could see an intersection at each end. He listened, he chose, and he moved. 

Reaching the next intersection, he applied the same practiced caution with the same results. He chose the same turn. He moved more quickly now, feeling slightly more secure in the quiet. The next intersection again, the same choice. This had been a square, he counted doors twice, then opened the door he had come out of originally. 

Looking in the room, he frowned. Confident that he'd moved in a square and certain of his count he had expected to still find traces of water where he had been sitting on the floor. This room looked untouched. He stepped out of the room and across the hall. Opening the door across the way, the same thing. Puzzled, he stepped back into the hall. He started down the hall, but after a few steps stopped. He turned and glanced back. A shiver ran down his spine, the hallway looked just as it had when he'd rounded the corner at the other end. 

Caution became a lower priority, he hurried back the way he came. Retracing his steps, he was confident he was back where he started. He opened the door he was sure he'd come out of originally, and stepped back into the room. Pristine. He opened the door, listened carefully, then stepped back out. Crossing the hallway, he checked the nearest door, it looked the same as all the others. He stepped back into the hallway. He looked down the hallway, no change. He turned around, looked the other way, no change. Closed doors as far as he could see, both ways.

He thought carefully, he knew he'd left doors open. He'd wanted to be able to listen. He'd left the door he thought the original room open to listen, but had to reopen it to leave the room. No click, no sound, just a closed door. He knew he didn't close the door of either room before looking down the hallway.

"Fuck." he whispered, staring down the hall, "...Twice. No, more than that. I've done this before, here." 

He moved again, taking the opposite turns from his previous choices. At least for the first few, the sameness of the hallways made it hard to keep track. Before he completely lost track, he dug through his pockets and found that at some point a fall had broken his pens. A bit more digging and he found a small bit of charcoal left from an art project. It was in a layer of charcoal dust, but enough was left to hold. He began drawing arrows pointing back the way he came. After counting ten turns, he turned around to try a different route. A few turns later, he noticed that the arrow was lighter than he remembered making it. Two more turns and it was missing. No smudges, no hints, just pristine walls.

He set off in another direction, trying again. The results were the same, only his bit of charcoal was almost gone. As he dug through his pockets for another pen, the watch on his wrist caught on the edge of the pocket. Pulling his hand out, he habitually glanced at the time and was reminded that the watch had been smashed.

"How long have I been here?" he wondered quietly. Thinking about it, he hadn't been hungry or thirsty. He'd been walking for a while, but he wasn't tired, rather he wasn't sleepy. His feet did hurt a bit though and bathroom breaks hadn't been a thing, which was good because no door yet opened had been for a bathroom.

He paused, then opened a door to yet another empty white room. Stopping in the doorway, he sat down to rest. His body wasn't really tired, but his mind needed a moment to process and recover as best it could. Slowly, he started going through his pockets. Sitting, his pants pockets were out of reach, but his jacket pockets weren't. He slowly took things out of his pocket and started lining them up. After the third object, he stopped, thought a moment and returned everything back to the pocket it came from.

"I'm going to have to be careful. If I set something down and look away, I may not get it back. This place already owes me a quarter." he mused out loud. 

"Let's see what I've got, versus what I think I've got." he continued.

He continued on, slowly looking at what he had. After a few minutes, he pulled out a battered Walkman with a radio builtin. 

He smiled, "Well, at least I can see who's around. Wonder if somebody's watching the lab rat."

He reached down and turned it on. Silence, not even static. He frowned and looked down - no red power light. He started digging through pockets again and eventually his hand came out with four AA batteries that he put into the Walkman. Absently, he set the dead batteries down. He paused what he was doing to just listen for a few moments. Still the same silence, broken by ventilation noise and the humming of florescent lights.

Turning on the player, he confirmed the power and put the headphones on. Slowly, focusing, he began to scan the dial, listening intently for anything other than static. Nothing but static, up and down the dial. He could never hear himself and he could usually tell when it wasn't work. This didn't feel like that, just empty. He could feel to a degree he never had before, the space around him. It was big, bigger than he had imagined so far. Most importantly, it was silent. That didn't mean that it was empty, just that there wasn't anybody he could listen in on, at least at the moment.

He sat for a few more minutes, listening to the silence and thinking. This meant something, he just wasn't sure what. Standing up again, he got ready to look around some more. Everything had a way out, he just had to find it. He looked down to clean up the mess he'd left, not wanting to leave much, if any trace of himself behind. Looking around, he groaned. The dead batteries were nowhere to be found. Nothing on his floor but the dark grey of his boots and pants. 

He started down the hall, trudging past familiar looking doors. Turning the corner, he stopped, turned around, and looked back the way he had come. Predictably, at this point, the door he'd left open was closed and, once again, no sign he'd passed through the hallway. 

Thinking for a moment, he dug through his pockets again, finding both a pencil and some chalk. He took a piece of chalk and carefully marked an arrow on the corner, pointing to the way he'd gone. Walking a little more quickly, he headed to the end of the hallway, turned around, then walked back. No mark.

No sign he'd been there. Like he didn't exist.

He walked for a while longer and realized he was humming. It took a moment to realized the song. He stopped again, listening. Still just the ventilation noise and the hum of bad fluorescent lights. He thought for a moment, looked at his broken watch again, put a chalk mark on his grey coat and quietly started signing.

"One hundred bottles of beer on the wall, one hundred bottles of beer..."

After counting down to one bottle of beer, he paused, made another mark and kept going, starting the song over. He'd been alternating turns to keep from just going in circles, now he could - sort of - keep track of time.

In an instant, he stumbled. Nothing but his own feet to trip on and he still managed to trip himself. Taking a deep breath, he opened a door and sat in the doorway again. That he could hide if he needed to was comforting, but he still wanted to keep an eye and ear out, just in case. He sat for a few more minutes, listening to slight background noises. His throat was a bit dry from singing, but he still hadn't found any water.

"Good thing I'm not actually thirsty." he said quietly to himself, "I wonder how many times I sang that damn song?"

Looking down to count the chalk marks, he realized he could barely see them on the light grey of his coat. Rummaging through his pockets, he pulled various small objects out of his pocket, smiling when he found the walkman he'd been using for one of his other tricks. Turning it on, he found it dead. He checked his pockets again, but couldn't find the spare batteries.

"Fuck." he suddenly whispered, staring down the hall, "...Twice.  I've done this before, but almost didn't remember."

He closed his eyes and walked his mind back through his day. A mostly normal day, work on a couple projects, two client calls, seeing what the Pigeon was seeing. A call from the junkyard, could he help with a project. Making a light dinner, getting geared up in his mission clothes and loading his pockets. His black clothes and black Doc Martens.

"Black, not grey." he mumbled, "I'm sure I dressed in black. The mirror showed me in black when I woke up here, didn't it?"




## Conclusion

Cautiously, he reached out for the sliver knob. Wrapping his hand gently around it, his palm covering the faint silver paw print centered on the knob. The knob turned easily, the door swinging silently opened. This was the first room with something notable in it. Before him, framed in repeating silver bands in an art deco pattern that arched across the top, was a full-size mirror. 

Rather than reflecting his image though, it acted as a window. The beige floor with silver borders and white chips in geometric patterns wasn't reflect either. Rather it was a similarly neutral floor with tiles, banded in navy or burgundy stripes, shaped into geometric patterns. Covered with a layer of familiar grime, it was the first familiar thing he'd seen. He could people moving in and out and simple benches pushed against the more ornate walls. Between the moving people, he could see the far side of the room. On a bench was a familiar cat, staring at him.

He smiled, relaxed his shoulders and stepped forward.
